# Successful Meetings: Product Requirements Document

## Page 1: The Problem

Successful Meetings addresses two related problems: meetings are held without a clear goal, and too many people spend too many hours in meetings that may not be needed.

### Problem 1: Meetings without a clear goal

Many meetings are scheduled without a defined target. When nobody states what the meeting should achieve, there is no way to measure afterwards whether it was useful or successful. Discussions drift, decisions are unclear, and the same topics return in later meetings.

### Problem 2: Too many hours lost to meetings

Employees are often invited to meetings they do not need to attend. For them the meeting is wasted time, and it also breaks their focus through context switching away from their actual work. For management, the cost multiplies: one meeting with many participants consumes many working hours. Management can see that large amounts of time go into meetings, but cannot tell which of those meetings are important enough to justify the cost.

### How things work today

- **Scheduling:** Meetings are created in Google Calendar. The invite holds a time, a title and a list of attendees.
- **Goal and agenda:** There is no dedicated place for the meeting's goal or agenda, so most meetings start without a stated target.
- **During the meeting:** Notes are not taken automatically. Nobody checks whether the meeting covered what it was meant to cover.
- **After the meeting:** There is no standard summary and no measure of whether the meeting succeeded. Nobody tracks whether each attendee actually took part.
- **Management view:** Management can see that many hours go into meetings but has no data on which meetings were useful.

### Who is affected

- **Employees** who are invited to meetings where their participation is not needed.
- **Managers and meeting organizers** who run meetings without a clear goal and cannot judge their success.
- **Company management** who see many hours spent in meetings and cannot assess their value.

The product is intended both for our own company and for external customers facing the same problems.

### Impact

Working hours that should go to employees' core jobs are spent in numerous meetings whose goal is not even clear. The organization pays for these hours twice: in the time spent in the meeting, and in the lost focus afterwards. Without clear goals and measurement, there is no basis for improving or cutting meetings.

### The cost of the problem

**Industry data.** A 2022 study by the University of North Carolina at Charlotte and Otter.ai surveyed 632 employees across 20 industries:

- Employees spend about **18 hours per week** in meetings. Managers spend about 22 hours.
- About **one third** of meeting time could be skipped if the same information were shared another way.
- Employees would prefer to decline **31%** of meeting invitations, but they actually decline only **14%**.
- Unnecessary meetings cost about **$25,000 per employee per year**, and about $42,000 per manager.
- For an organization of 5,000 employees, the waste is about **$101 million per year**.

**Example: a 100-employee company.**

A single meeting with 4 attendees, 30 minutes long, at an average cost of $40 per hour:
4 attendees x 0.5 hours x $40 = **$80 per meeting**. If one of the four attendees did not need to be there, $20 (25%) of that meeting is wasted.

Across the company (100 employees, $40 per hour, 48 working weeks, one third of meeting time unnecessary):

| Meeting hours per employee per week | Equivalent $80 meetings per week | Yearly meeting cost | Yearly cost of unnecessary meetings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 250 | $960,000 | $320,000 |
| 10 | 500 | $1,920,000 | $640,000 |
| 18 (study average) | 900 | $3,456,000 | $1,152,000 |

Formula: employees x weekly meeting hours x hourly cost x 48 weeks.
